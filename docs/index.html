What can happen next

  1. Get to a safe place. A friend, relative, church, clinic or shelter.
  2. Get medical care. Go as soon as you can. If you were raped, reaching a clinic or hospital within 72 hours lets you get medicine that can prevent HIV, plus emergency contraception.
  3. Report when you are ready. You can speak to the police or a one-stop centre. You do not have to wash or change before you report.
  4. Ask about a protection order. A court can order the abuser to stay away. A counsellor can walk you through it.

How a case moves

  1. The agent confirms you are safe and gives a reference number.
  2. With your consent, they pass the report to police, a clinic or a shelter near you.
  3. They log each step with the time, so there is a record if something happens.
  4. They follow up until a service has taken over.

Know the signs. Act safely.

Signs of gender-based violence

Physical: unexplained bruises, injuries or "accidents".
Emotional: fear, anxiety, shame, low confidence, being cut off from friends and family.
Control: checking a phone, tracking movements, taking money or ID, stopping work or school.
Sexual: pressure or force, refusing protection, or threats if you say no.
Children: sudden changes in behaviour, fear of a person, bedwetting, falling grades.

What causes it

Violence is a choice made by the person using it. It is never the victim's fault. It grows where power is unequal and abuse is excused: beliefs that men should control women, poverty and money stress, alcohol and drugs, having seen violence as a child, weak punishment for offenders, and silence from families and communities. Anyone can be a victim, including men, boys and LGBTQ+ people.

How to react

If it is happening to you: get to safety, call a helpline, see a nurse or doctor, and keep notes.
If someone tells you: listen, believe them, say it is not their fault, and offer options without pushing.
If you see it: do not confront an angry person alone. Call the police, a neighbour or a helpline.
If you cause harm: step away and ask for help to change. Men's counselling services exist.

Abuse is more than hitting

It can be controlling your money, phone or movements, threats, insults, forcing sex, or keeping you from family and work.

If a friend tells you

Listen. Believe them. Say it is not their fault. Share this page only if it is safe, and let them decide what to do.

Create a safety plan

Pick a place to go and a person to call. Keep ID, medicine, money and a charged phone in one spot. Agree a code word with a trusted friend.

Online safety

Change passwords, turn off location sharing, and check for apps you did not install. Clear your browser history after visiting help pages.

For communities

Schools, churches, taxi ranks and chiefs' councils can host talks, name a trusted contact person, and refuse to excuse abuse as "family business".

Your privacy

What we do

No account. No name. No tracking. We do not send what you write anywhere. Your record is encrypted on your own device.

What the live app will add

Partner-verified services with last-checked dates, a data policy built on consent, only the data needed, no sharing without your say-so, and anonymous hotspot statistics for prevention. Any report shared with a service happens only when you choose it.
